# Rules behind `decide`

A digest of the sources, checked on 17 October 2026. Not legal advice. The Commission's guidelines
on Article 50 were still pending at that date and may refine these points.

## AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)

- **Article 50(4), deep fakes.** Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates image, audio
  or video content constituting a deep fake must disclose that the content has been artificially
  generated or manipulated. For an evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous
  work, the duty is limited to disclosing it in a way that does not hamper the display or enjoyment
  of the work. Use authorised by law to detect, prevent, investigate or prosecute criminal offences
  is exempt.
- **Article 50(4), text.** Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates text published to
  inform the public on matters of public interest must disclose it, unless the text has undergone
  human review or editorial control and a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility.
- **Article 3(60), deep fake:** AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video content that
  resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a
  person to be authentic or truthful.
- **Article 50(2)** puts machine-readable marking on providers of generative AI systems, and exempts
  systems that perform "an assistive function for standard editing" or do not substantially alter
  the input. Article 50(4) has no such wording for deployers; `decide` treats standard edits
  (colour, noise, spelling) as not "generated or manipulated" under Article 3(60), which is a
  reading, not a quoted exception.
- **Article 50(5).** The information must be given in a clear and distinguishable manner at the
  latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure, and must conform to the applicable
  accessibility requirements.
- Article 50 applies from **2 August 2026**.

## EU icons (European Commission, 10 June 2026)

Source: <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-icons-labelling-ai-generated-content>

- Three icons, each in four colour variants (black, white, black 50% transparent, white 50%
  transparent):
  - **Basic icon:** AI was involved in a deep fake or published text, or a custom text label or an
    interactive second layer is used.
  - **Fully AI-generated:** the whole deep fake or text is generated by AI, with no human-created
    elements or editorial control apart from prompting.
  - **Partially AI-modified:** pre-existing human-made content partly modified with AI.
- "The use of these EU icons is optional, but the labelling requirements under Article 50 AI Act are
  not." Using the icons "does not establish legal compliance by itself."
- The icons "are made publicly available for everyone to use freely, without the need for
  attribution to the Commission or the AI Office." Use by non-signatories of the Code "should not be
  construed as signaling of their adherence to the code."
- Placement: "clearly perceivable and distinguishable at the latest at the time of first exposure";
  "directly embedded into the deepfake or published text (except for creative works)", unless
  alternatives such as a user interface overlay are available; it "must be visible when content is
  reshared or downloaded"; placed "where no intervening overlay elements exist"; "in a clearly
  visible size".
- Text labels: plain language, avoiding "jargon, confusing wording and abbreviations other than
  'AI'". The basic icon performed better with a text label in user testing.
- Accessibility measures the page encourages: alt text or ARIA labels where possible; temporary
  disclosures that "remain visible long enough to be read and understood"; an interactive second
  layer that "shall be navigable using assistive technologies".

## Code of Practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content (10 June 2026)

Source: <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content>.
A voluntary tool. As summarised by Freshfields
(<https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/eu-ai-act-unpacked-33-the-final-code-of-practice-on-transparency-of-ai-generate-102n4yx>):
a label should allow immediate recognition without user interaction, stay visible for a sufficient
duration, be embedded in the content unless an equivalent alternative exists, and be perceivable at
the latest at first exposure. Audio-only content needs an audible disclaimer. An equivalent icon or
label may replace the EU icon if it meets the same design and placement specifications. The final
Code dropped the first draft's split between "fully AI-generated" and "AI-assisted" for marking.

## How the tool maps these rules

| Rule | What ai-content-labels does |
|---|---|
| Embedded, visible after download and reshare | `stamp` burns the label into the pixels |
| Perceivable at first exposure, clearly visible size | Stamp height about 4.5% of the shorter side (at least 28 px), top-left by default; shrunk only to fit a narrow image, and refused below 20 px |
| Plain-language text label | Text next to the basic EU icon in the content's language |
| Accessibility (Article 50(5), WCAG) | 4.5:1 text contrast on any backdrop, `role="img"` and `aria-label` in the SVG, alt text returned by every command |
| Audio-only | `decide` tells you to add an audible disclaimer |
| Machine-readable marking | An IPTC DigitalSourceType XMP tag in stamped files. Article 50(2) puts machine-readable marking on providers of generative systems, not on deployers; the tag is a courtesy, not a substitute |
