# Rules behind `decide`

A digest of the sources, checked on 17 October 2026. Not legal advice. The Commission's Article 50
Guidelines (20 July 2026) are its first interpretation and will be reviewed as practice develops
(Guidelines para 155).

## AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)

- **Article 50(4), deep fakes.** Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates image, audio
  or video content constituting a deep fake must disclose that the content has been artificially
  generated or manipulated. For an evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous
  work, the duty is limited to disclosing it in a way that does not hamper the display or enjoyment
  of the work. Use authorised by law to detect, prevent, investigate or prosecute criminal offences
  is exempt.
- **Article 50(4), text.** Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates text published to
  inform the public on matters of public interest must disclose it, unless the text has undergone
  human review or editorial control and a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility.
- **Article 3(60), deep fake:** AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video content that
  resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a
  person to be authentic or truthful.
- **Article 2(10).** The Act does not apply to the obligations of deployers who are natural persons
  using AI systems in the course of a purely personal, non-professional activity.
- **Article 50(2)** puts machine-readable marking on providers of generative AI systems, and exempts
  systems that perform "an assistive function for standard editing" or do not substantially alter
  the input. Article 50(4) has no such wording for deployers; `decide` treats standard edits
  (colour, noise, spelling) as not "generated or manipulated" under Article 3(60), which is a
  reading, not a quoted exception. The Guidelines describe manipulation as alteration beyond
  standard editing (para 59), explain standard editing in para 90, and discuss minor manipulations
  of images, audio and video in para 116.
- **Article 50(5).** The information must be given in a clear and distinguishable manner at the
  latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure, and must conform to the applicable
  accessibility requirements.
- Article 50 applies from **2 August 2026**.

## Commission Guidelines on Article 50 (20 July 2026)

Source: <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems>,
PDF: <https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215> (C(2026) 5054 final, Annex:
"Guidelines on the implementation of the transparency obligations for certain AI systems under
Article 50 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689"). Paragraph numbers below were checked against that PDF.

- **Who must label (paras 12-20).** The deployer: whoever uses the AI system under their authority
  in a professional activity. The obligation binds deployers in the EU, and deployers established
  elsewhere when they foresee the content being used in the EU, for example by publishing it on
  the open internet (Article 2(1); para 13). Employees acting for a company are not separate
  deployers; the company is (para 14). Platforms and others who only disseminate content
  generated by third parties are not deployers, but are encouraged to keep the labels (para 16).
  Purely personal, non-professional use by an individual is outside the Act (paras 19-20);
  activity for pay or regular economic benefit, including freelance work, is professional
  (para 19).
- **Deep fake criteria (paras 112-116).** Four cumulative criteria: resemblance, existing, persons,
  objects, places, entities or events, and false appearance of authenticity (para 113). "Existing"
  covers subjects that exist, can plausibly exist or could plausibly have existed; dragons or
  humans flying unaided fall outside (para 113). The assessment is objective and looks at the whole
  context and the foreseeable audience; photorealism alone is not decisive (para 114). It must
  account for children, the elderly and people with lower AI literacy when they are a foreseeable
  part of the audience (para 115).
- **Minor manipulations (para 116).** Removing a passer-by, lighting, colour correction or noise
  reduction may not make content a deep fake; it depends on context.
- **Machine-readable marking is not enough (para 117).** The disclosure must be perceivable without
  technical tools or dedicated actions.
- **Creative works (paras 119-123).** The categories are read strictly and must be evident to the
  audience (para 122). Content that is exclusively informative or commercial and recognisable as
  such (for example news) is excluded; advertisements or documentaries may count only in specific
  situations; where informative and creative character mix, the informative one prevails
  (para 122). The disclosure is still required, in a way that does not hamper the work (para 123).
- **Text (paras 130-138).** "Published" means open to an indeterminate number of readers; internal
  and private correspondence is not published (para 131). Human review means a deliberate check of
  the substance by people with relevant knowledge, with fact-checking as a minimum (para 134).
  Spell-checks, the mere existence of an editorial policy, automated review or cursory approval do
  not count (para 135). Substantive AI changes after sign-off void the exception (para 136). The
  identity and contact details of whoever holds editorial responsibility should be publicly
  available, for example in legal information or a colophon (para 138).
- **Article 50(5) (paras 141-144).** Clear means noticeable, easy to understand and accessible,
  including for persons with disabilities (para 142). The duty applies to each output and each
  person's first exposure (para 143). Where people may foreseeably not see content from its
  beginning, a disclosure only at the beginning "does not adequately inform those persons and
  should be complemented with disclosure at later moments, where possible" (para 143). Article 50 adds no accessibility requirements of its own; the
  existing ones apply (para 144).
- **Dates (paras 153-154).** Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026 (para 153). Deep fakes generated
  or manipulated before that date need not be labelled retroactively. Text generated before that
  date but published on or after it must be labelled (para 154). The AI Omnibus transitional
  period to 2 December 2026 covers only providers' marking under Article 50(2) for systems already
  on the market; it does not delay Article 50(4) (para 153).
- **Review (para 155).** The Guidelines are a first interpretation and will be reviewed.

## EU icons (European Commission, 10 June 2026)

Source: <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-icons-labelling-ai-generated-content>

- Three icons, each in four colour variants (black, white, black 50% transparent, white 50%
  transparent):
  - **Basic icon:** AI was involved in a deep fake or published text, or a custom text label or an
    interactive second layer is used.
  - **Fully AI-generated:** the whole deep fake or text is generated by AI, with no human-created
    elements or editorial control apart from prompting.
  - **Partially AI-modified:** pre-existing human-made content partly modified with AI.
- "The use of these EU icons is optional, but the labelling requirements under Article 50 AI Act are
  not." Using the icons "does not establish legal compliance by itself."
- The icons "are made publicly available for everyone to use freely, without the need for
  attribution to the Commission or the AI Office." Use by non-signatories of the Code "should not be
  construed as signaling of their adherence to the code."
- Placement: "clearly perceivable and distinguishable at the latest at the time of first exposure";
  "directly embedded into the deepfake or published text (except for creative works)", unless
  alternatives such as a user interface overlay are available; it "must be visible when content is
  reshared or downloaded"; placed "where no intervening overlay elements exist"; "in a clearly
  visible size".
- Text labels: plain language, avoiding "jargon, confusing wording and abbreviations other than
  'AI'". The basic icon performed better with a text label in user testing.
- Accessibility measures the page encourages: alt text or ARIA labels where possible; temporary
  disclosures that "remain visible long enough to be read and understood"; an interactive second
  layer that "shall be navigable using assistive technologies".
- The human-made mark in this project (a hand in the same disc) is not part of the EU set and has
  no meaning under the AI Act. A false "human-made" claim in business communication can be a
  misleading action under the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC (Article 6),
  separately from the AI Act.

## Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content (10 June 2026)

Source: <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content>,
PDF: <https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/129555>. A voluntary tool; its Section 2
applies to signatories as deployers. Assessed as adequate by the Commission (opinion of 8 July
2026, published 9 July: it "adequately covers the obligations provided for in Articles 50(2), (4)
and (5)") and by the AI Board on 9 July 2026:
<https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-opinion-assessment-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content>.
The icons page calls it the Code of Practice on marking and labelling of AI-generated content.
Adherence is voluntary; non-signatories are bound by Article 50 directly, and using the icons does
not make anyone a signatory.

- **Measure 1.1 (design).** The icon or an equivalent label carries the capital letters "AI";
  signatories are encouraged to add whether the content is generated or modified, for example as
  text next to the icon (1.1(b)). Sizes and styles may vary as long as the label stays clear,
  accessible and distinguishable (1.1(c)). Where visual disclosure is not possible, such as
  audio-only content, a short audible disclaimer in plain language goes at the beginning.
- **Sub-measure 1.2.1 (placement).** Immediate recognition without user action (a), visible long
  enough to be noticed (b), embedded in the content unless an equivalent alternative such as an
  overlay exists (c), perceivable at first exposure and against any background (d).
- **Sub-measure 1.2.2 (visual placement).** No intervening overlay elements, for example the top
  right corner of an image or video (a). For video, signatories "will display the icon or
  equivalent label at the beginning of the video as well as, where possible, at regular intervals
  throughout the video and, at a minimum, after interruptions (e.g., after commercial or
  advertising breaks)"; they are "furthermore encouraged" to display it throughout the deep fake
  video or during the deep fake part (b). Keeping it on screen for the whole video is this
  project's recommendation, not the Code's baseline. For published text, above or at the top of
  the text, near the headline, or in the colophon (f).
- **Sub-measure 1.2.3 (audio).** Where visual disclosure is not possible, an audible disclaimer at
  the latest at first exposure; for long-form or live audio, "where appropriate", reminders at
  regular intervals and at minimum after interruptions.
- **Measure 2.1 (internal compliance process).** Signatories keep internal processes and
  documentation of how they implement the disclosures. Publishing is only encouraged:
  "Signatories are encouraged to publish information on the disclosure solution they apply", and
  for their own icon or label "a similar description of the icon or label is encouraged to be made
  publicly available", which may include images and the meaning of each label. This project
  applies that encouragement to the human-made mark.
- **Commitment 3 (creative works).** Disclosure that does not hamper the work but is clear,
  distinguishable, accessible and given at first exposure, for example in accompanying notes, the
  description or the opening or end credits; on websites and apps it may sit outside but adjacent
  to the frame, as long as nobody has to act to see it.
- **Commitment 4 (editorial review).** Deployers outside the media sector keep a policy that names
  the person with editorial responsibility (name, role, contact) and publish those contact details.

## How the tool maps these rules

| Rule | What ai-content-labels does |
|---|---|
| Embedded, visible after download and reshare (Code Sub-measure 1.2.1(c)) | `stamp` burns the label into the pixels |
| Perceivable at first exposure, clearly visible size (Code Sub-measure 1.2.1(a) and (d)) | The smallest legible stamp: 24 px tall with text, 20 px minimal, as the image is shown full width on a phone (390 CSS px), never below that in the file or above 512 px; refused when the image is too small for it. Placed automatically in the least invasive free spot: the bottom-right corner, else bottom centre or bottom left, else the top edge (the Code's example is the top right corner). Any corner meets 1.2.2(a) as long as nothing covers the label; `--position` overrides it per platform |
| Sizes and styles may vary if the label stays clear (Code Measure 1.1(c)) | Optional label colour (`--color`, or `auto` from the image's accent) on an opaque pill, with white or dark text that keeps 4.5:1; the EU disc stays in its official black or white |
| Plain-language text with "generated" or "modified" (Code Measure 1.1(b)) | Text next to the basic EU icon in the content's language |
| Accessibility (Article 50(5), Guidelines para 142, WCAG) | 4.5:1 text contrast on any backdrop, `role="img"`, `lang` and `aria-label` in the SVG, real text with `render --format html`, alt text and the label sentence returned by every command |
| Audio-only (Code Measure 1.1, audio; Sub-measure 1.2.3) | `decide` tells you to add an audible disclaimer at the start, in the language of the recording, and to repeat it in long or live recordings |
| Video seen from the middle (Code Sub-measure 1.2.2(b); Guidelines para 143) | `decide` tells you to show the label from the start, repeat it at regular intervals where possible and at least after breaks, as the Code asks. Keeping it for the whole video, which the Code encourages, is this project's recommendation |
| Editorial review decides public-interest text (Guidelines paras 133-138) | `decide` asks `--editorial-review` before it gives a verdict for public-interest text |
| Creative works (Article 50(4), Code Commitment 3) | `decide --creative` moves the label next to the work, into the description or the credits |
| Editorial review of text (Guidelines paras 133-138, Code Commitment 4) | `decide --editorial-review` asks you to publish who holds editorial responsibility |
| Machine-readable marking | An IPTC DigitalSourceType XMP tag in files stamped with a generated or modified label, plus the project's own marker in every stamped file so it is not stamped twice. Article 50(2) puts machine-readable marking on providers of generative systems, not on deployers, and it does not replace the visible label (Guidelines para 117); the tag is a courtesy |
